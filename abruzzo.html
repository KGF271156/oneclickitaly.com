Abruzzo — mountains to the Adriatic.

Europe's highest Apennine peaks tumbling down to a quiet Adriatic coastline — bears, castles and some of Italy's most underrated cooking.

What makes Abruzzo, Abruzzo

Gran Sasso & the Apennines

The highest peaks on mainland Italy south of the Alps, home to the Marsican brown bear and the Campo Imperatore plateau — nicknamed 'Italy's Tibet'.

The Trabocchi Coast

Spindly wooden fishing platforms (trabocchi) stilted out over the Adriatic, many now restaurants serving the day's catch above the waves.

Hill towns & castles

Santo Stefano di Sessanio, Rocca Calascio and dozens of stone borghi that feel barely touched by the last few centuries.

Events

Worth planning around

Perdonanza Celestiniana

28–29 August — L'Aquila's centuries-old forgiveness festival, a UNESCO-listed tradition.

Panarda

Traditional marathon feast of up to 30 courses, still hosted in villages across the region.

Majella wolf howls

Autumn — guided evening treks to hear wolves in Majella National Park.

Guardiagrele artisan fairs

Year-round — goldsmiths and ironworkers continuing a centuries-old craft tradition.

Abruzzese abroad

The diaspora carries it on

Abruzzese emigration shaped entire neighbourhoods abroad — from Pennsylvania's coal towns to Buenos Aires — with mutual aid societies and feast-day processions still running a century on.
