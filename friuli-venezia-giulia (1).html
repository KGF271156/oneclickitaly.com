Friuli-Venezia Giulia — where Italy, Austria and Slovenia meet.

A borderland region of Habsburg coffeehouses, Roman ruins and crisp white wine, shaped by centuries of shifting frontiers.

What makes Friuli-Venezia Giulia, Friuli-Venezia Giulia

Trieste's coffee culture

A former Habsburg port city with grand coffeehouses, Mitteleuropean architecture and a character unlike anywhere else in Italy.

Aquileia's Roman ruins

Once one of the largest cities in the Roman Empire, now a UNESCO site with the largest Roman mosaic floor still in its original location.

A three-border region

Italian, Slovenian, German and Friulian are all spoken here — a genuine crossroads culture shaped by centuries of shifting rule.

Worth planning around

Barcolana

October — Trieste's giant sailing regatta, one of the world's largest.

Festa di San Giusto

3 November — Trieste's patron saint day, marked across the old city.

Udine Friuli DOC

September — a huge regional food and wine festival filling Udine's streets.

Mittelfest

Summer — Cividale del Friuli's festival of Central European performing arts.

Friulian abroad

The diaspora carries it on

Friulian emigration ran heavily toward building and stonemasonry trades abroad — from Canada's construction industry to South America — a craft reputation the diaspora still carries with pride.
