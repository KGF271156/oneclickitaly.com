Molise — Italy's quietest region.

So small and so quiet that there's a running Italian joke that Molise doesn't exist — ancient shepherd trails, untouched hill towns, and barely a tourist in sight.

What makes Molise, Molise

The tratturi

Ancient grass-covered shepherd trails, some over 100 metres wide, once used to drive flocks between summer and winter pasture — now protected as cultural heritage.

A region apart

Only split from Abruzzo in 1963, Molise remains Italy's least-visited mainland region — which is precisely its appeal for those who find it.

Samnite & Roman roots

Pre-Roman Samnite ruins at Pietrabbondante sit largely unexcavated and almost entirely unvisited, a rarity in Italy.

Worth planning around

Mysteries of Campobasso

Corpus Christi — enormous metal 'flying' sculptures carried through the streets by hidden bearers.

Sagra della Pampanella

Winter — village festivals celebrating the region's signature spiced pork dish.

Palio di Larino

Summer — a historic costumed horse race through Larino's old town.

Transumanza re-enactments

Spring/autumn — walking the ancient tratturi alongside shepherds and their flocks.

Molisan abroad

The diaspora carries it on

Despite its size, Molise sent a remarkable share of its population abroad in the early 20th century, and Molisan heritage societies remain active well out of proportion to the region's modest population today.
