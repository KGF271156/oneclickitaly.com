What makes Aosta Valley, Aosta Valley

Mont Blanc & the Matterhorn

Western Europe's highest peak and one of the world's most recognisable mountains both sit on this region's borders — a magnet for climbers and skiers alike.

A valley of castles

Over 100 castles and fortified towers line the Dora Baltea valley, from Fénis's fairy-tale turrets to Sarre's royal hunting lodge.

Bilingual by law

Italian and French share equal official status here, a legacy of centuries under the House of Savoy — road signs and school lessons run in both.

Events

Worth planning around

Batailles de Reines

Spring–autumn — gentle 'cow fighting' contests where Valdostan cattle jostle for herd dominance, not visitors.

Sant'Orso Fair

30–31 January — a 1,000-year-old craft fair filling Aosta's streets with woodworkers and artisans.

Tor des Géants

September — a brutal 330km mountain ultramarathon circling the entire region.

Carnevale Storico di Verrès

February — a Renaissance-costumed carnival re-enacting the court of Caterina di Challant.

Valdostan abroad

The diaspora carries it on

Valdostan emigration ran heavily toward Alpine and mining regions abroad — Wales, the French Alps, Argentina's Patagonia — carrying mountain-guiding and dairy skills with it.
