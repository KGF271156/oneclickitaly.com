Apulia — the heel of the boot.

Sun-bleached trulli, baroque Lecce and a coastline stretching from the Adriatic to the Ionian — Italy's underrated south at its most alive.

What makes Apulia, Apulia

The trulli of Alberobello

Whitewashed, cone-roofed stone houses unique to this corner of Italy, clustered by the thousand in a UNESCO-listed old town.

Baroque Lecce

Nicknamed the 'Florence of the South' for its extravagant, honey-coloured baroque architecture carved from local limestone.

Salento's two seas

The region's southern tip is narrow enough to watch the sun set over the Ionian and rise over the Adriatic in the same day.

Events

Worth planning around

Notte della Taranta

August — Europe's largest folk music festival, built around the hypnotic pizzica tarantata dance.

Festa di San Nicola

7–9 May — Bari's patron saint festival, with a statue procession out to sea by boat.

Carnevale di Putignano

One of Europe's oldest carnivals, running for weeks with satirical floats.

Fiera del Levante

September — Bari's major trade and culture fair, running since 1930.

Apulian abroad

The diaspora carries it on

Apulian communities abroad — from Toronto's Little Italy to Melbourne's inner suburbs — remain some of the most tightly knit of the Italian diaspora, with feast-day processions and social clubs still thriving generations on.
